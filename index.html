The Scale of Everything

The observable universe spans 93 billion light-years across. Within it, two trillion galaxies drift through an expanding void — each one home to hundreds of billions of stars, most with their own planets. We are an impossibly small speck reflecting on impossibly large questions.

HUBBLE SPACE TELESCOPE · 1995 / 2014

Pillars of Creation

Towering columns of gas and dust inside the Eagle Nebula — stellar nurseries where new suns are born from collapsing molecular clouds, 6,500 light-years from Earth.

OUR GALAXY

The Milky Way

A barred spiral galaxy 100,000 light-years across, home to 200–400 billion stars. Our solar system orbits the galactic center at 828,000 km/h — completing one revolution every 225 million years.

HUBBLE ULTRA DEEP FIELD · 2004

10,000 Galaxies in One Frame

A single exposure of a tiny, seemingly empty patch of sky revealed 10,000 galaxies — some dating back to just 800 million years after the Big Bang. The universe is not empty. It is filled beyond imagination.

STELLAR REMNANT

The Crab Nebula

The debris cloud of a supernova explosion observed by Chinese astronomers in 1054 AD. At its heart pulses a neutron star — a city-sized sphere of collapsed matter spinning 30 times every second.

The Cosmic Codex

Four forces that shape everything we know — and everything we don't.

◉ 0 Black Holes — Sagittarius A* The supermassive black hole anchoring our galaxy's core. Four million times the mass of our Sun, its gravitational pull warps spacetime so severely that even light cannot escape once it crosses the event horizon.
◇ 0 Dark Matter An invisible scaffolding that binds galaxies together. Undetectable by any instrument yet devised, it exerts gravitational force on everything around it — comprising 27% of the universe's total mass-energy content.
✧ 0 Confirmed Exoplanets Worlds orbiting distant suns, many within habitable zones where liquid water could pool on rocky surfaces. Each discovery expands the frontier of where complex chemistry — and perhaps life — might take root.
∞ 13.8B Years — The Age of Time Itself The Big Bang ignited 13.8 billion years ago, forging hydrogen and helium in minutes. Every heavy element in your body was later synthesized inside a dying star and scattered across the cosmos by its final explosion.

Into the Dark

Dark energy is accelerating the universe's expansion — a repulsive force embedded in the fabric of space itself, growing stronger as the cosmos stretches. In 100 billion years, every galaxy beyond our local group will have vanished beyond the cosmic horizon, forever unreachable.

Light takes 8 minutes to reach us from the Sun. 4 years from the nearest star. 2.5 million years from Andromeda. The universe you see is not the universe that exists — it is a fossil record. You are always looking into the past. The present is unknowable.

The Human Element

Since the dawn of consciousness, we have looked up. From cave paintings of star patterns to the James Webb Deep Field — our species is defined by the compulsion to understand what lies beyond the horizon. Space exploration is not a luxury. It is who we are.

Where Astronauts Meet the Infinite

Since Yuri Gagarin's first orbit in 1961, over 600 humans have ventured beyond Earth's atmosphere. Each one has described the experience as profoundly life-changing — a phenomenon scientists call the "Overview Effect."

From the International Space Station, astronauts witness 16 sunrises every 24 hours, orbiting at 28,000 km/h. They see no borders, no nations — only a fragile blue marble suspended in an ocean of darkness.
